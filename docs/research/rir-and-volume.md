# RIR, effort and volume: what Nippard and the studies he cites say

Research date: 2026-11-22. Confidence labels: **[primary]** = read the source itself (Nippard's own site, PubMed or Crossref abstract). **[secondary]** = read only through press coverage, search snippets or unofficial program copies. **[unconfirmed]** = could not verify.

Access limits: jeffnippard.com product pages returned HTTP 429 throughout, and the paid program PDFs (Pure Bodybuilding, Min-Max, Fundamentals) are behind a paywall. I did not use the pirated copies on Scribd or Studocu as sources. Nippard's YouTube uploads in 2026 (checked through the channel RSS feed) are mostly about nutrition and lifestyle. His newest material on proximity to failure is from 2024–2025.

## Bottom line

1. **RIR target.** Nippard's current baseline (blog, May 2024) is to end sets at **0–3 RIR**. Intermediate and advanced lifters should take most sets to **1–2 RIR**, and beginners to 1–3. He takes the **last set to failure (0 RIR)** on most exercises but **not on squats or deadlifts**. In 2025 he moved to taking the **first** set to failure and then judging RIR on the later sets [secondary]. His programs start with an easier intro week (about RPE 7–9) and build to RPE 9–10, with failure on most last sets [secondary]. He does not give a separate RIR per muscle group.
2. **RIR and volume.** No Nippard source and no cited study gives a rule like "you hit 0 RIR, so cut sets next session". What the evidence does show:
   - Failure causes more rep loss within a session (Refalo 2024). Total volume over 8 weeks was still the same.
   - Nippard pairs lower volume with higher effort (Min-Max uses 1–2 sets, often to failure). His deload is "one fewer set per exercise and a few reps further from failure".
   - RP's autoregulation adds or removes sets based on **soreness, pump and performance**, not on RIR alone, and lowers the RIR target every week or two.
   
   A defensible app rule is to hold sets after a near-failure session and only reduce them if performance drops or soreness hasn't healed.
3. **"80% effort".** Nippard does not use this term; I found no instance of it. There are two reasonable ways to translate it:
   - **(a) RPE 8 = 2 RIR**, using the RIR-based RPE scale (Zourdos 2016). This gives the same target at every rep range.
   - **(b) 80% of the reps you could do to failure**, so RIR ≈ 0.25 × reps done:
     - 6–8 reps → about 1.5–2 RIR
     - 8–12 reps → about 2–3 RIR
     - 12–20 reps → about 3–5 RIR
   
   The 12–20 case under (b) goes past Nippard's 3-RIR ceiling, and lifters tend to underestimate RIR most on high-rep sets. **Recommendation:** read "80% effort" as **about 2 RIR (RPE 8)**, capped at **3 RIR** for 12–20 rep sets. That keeps it inside Nippard's 1–2 RIR / 0–3 RIR window.
4. **Weekly volume.**
   - In 2023 Nippard said "10–20 sets per body part per week" for most people.
   - In April 2024 he split it by training age:
     - Beginners: 4–8 sets, working up to 8–12
     - Intermediates: 8–15
     - Advanced: 12–20, with 15–25 for one or two lagging muscles
   - Pelland et al. (2025/2026, Sports Medicine) found hypertrophy keeps rising with volume, with diminishing returns. They count an indirect set as **0.5 set**.
   
   The app's 10–20 range fits. In his writing Nippard ties volume to effort only indirectly: he says sets should stop "no more than two or three reps in the tank", and his low-volume programs rely on failure. His volume article (2024) does not define what counts as a hard set.

## Q1. Proximity to failure: what does Nippard recommend?

**General target (2024) [primary]**
Source: "How Hard Should You Train to Build Muscle?", jeffnippard.com blog, 13 May 2024 (companion to the YouTube video of the same name). https://jeffnippard.com/blogs/news/how-hard-should-you-train-to-build-muscle
- You are "likely to make your best gains" finishing sets with **0–3 RIR**. If most sets end with more than 3 RIR, you are likely leaving gains on the table.
- **Beginners (under 1 year):** 1–3 RIR on most sets. They should focus on technique on compounds and can try failure on simple machine or cable exercises at the end of a workout.
- **Intermediate and advanced:** most working sets at **1–2 RIR**. The **last set of each exercise goes to failure, except squats and deadlifts** ("unless I'm doing deadlifts or squats, I almost always take my last set of each exercise to failure").
- Studies cited:
  - Robinson et al. 2024 meta-regression
  - Refalo et al. 2024 (J Sports Sci): 1–2 RIR gave the same quadriceps growth as failure in trained lifters

**Earlier baseline (2023) [primary]**
Source: "How To Build Muscle (Explained In 5 Levels)", jeffnippard.com, 9 Jul 2023. https://jeffnippard.com/blogs/news/how-to-build-muscle-explained-in-5-levels
- "You shouldn't be leaving more than two or three reps in the tank for most of your sets."
- Leaving 5 or more RIR "does make sense for recovery, especially when training primarily for strength."

**By exercise type [primary + secondary]**
- His only explicit split by exercise is avoiding failure on squats and deadlifts (and, for beginners, on technical compounds), versus failure on low-risk machine, cable and isolation work. Source: the 2024 blog above.
- A secondary summary of his "effective reps" video gives 1–3 RIR for compounds and failure or near-failure for high-rep single-joint work. Source: https://deadspin.com/10-training-tips-i-learned-from-jeff-nippard-that-actually-made-a-difference/ [secondary].
- **By muscle group:** I found no Nippard guidance [unconfirmed / appears not to exist].

**By set position: the 2025 change to first-set failure [secondary]**
- He used to leave 1–2 RIR on the early sets and go to failure on the last. He now takes the **first** working set to failure: "when I do my failure set first I would get like two or three extra reps", and afterwards "now you know what your zero reps in reserve feels like, so you can actually gauge it better".
- Sources:
  - YouTube Short "Why I Take My First Set To Failure Now" (date not confirmed)
  - The Jeff Nippard Podcast, "This Study Completely Changed How I Train To Failure", with Dr. Martin Refalo (October; the year looks like 2025 but is not confirmed): https://icecream4prs.libsyn.com/this-study-completely-changed-how-train-to-failure
  - Coverage: Men's Journal / Yahoo, 10 Nov 2026, https://www.mensjournal.com/fitness/natural-bodybuilder-why-he-takes-his-first-set-to-muscular-failure-for-maximum-gains
- **Min-Max Phase 2:** the official product page says most exercises are now taken to failure on the **first** working set instead of the last. Min-Max (Phase 1) uses 1–2 hard sets per exercise, often to failure. URLs: https://jeffnippard.com/products/min-max-program-phase-2 and https://jeffnippard.com/products/the-min-max-program. [secondary: seen in search snippets of the official pages; direct fetch was blocked by HTTP 429]
- Related study: Hermann et al. 2025, "Without Fail", Med Sci Sports Exerc 57(9):2021–2031, PMID 40249908 [primary abstract]. Design: 42 trained people did one set of 9 exercises, twice a week for 8 weeks, either to failure or at 2 RIR. Failure "tended to favor" some hypertrophy measures, but the absolute differences were modest. Strength and endurance were similar.

**By week in the block (Pure Bodybuilding) [secondary]**
- The program splits working sets into **Early Sets** (all but the final set) and the **Last Set**. Early sets are "a little bit easier (slightly lower RPE)". The last set "is pushed to failure on all exercises (besides intro/deload weeks)". Some phases say "most (but not all)".
- The first week is an intro or deload week at about **RPE 7–9 (1–3 RIR)**. After that, most sets are at RPE 9–10.
- These details come from Boostcamp listings and program summaries, not the official guide:
  - https://www.boostcamp.app/users/kLHRYu-nippards-the-bodybuilding-transformation-system
  - BarBend, last updated 28 May 2025: https://barbend.com/news/jeff-nippard-pure-bodybuilding-program/
- Exact per-week RPE tables are [unconfirmed against the official PDF].

**What the cited research says [primary abstracts]**
- **Robinson et al. 2024**, Sports Med 54(9):2209–2231, PMID 38970765 (doi 10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2). Across 55 studies with RIR estimated, hypertrophy "improves as sets are terminated closer to failure", while strength was "similar across a wide range of RIR". The authors call the analysis exploratory because the RIR values were estimated.
- **Refalo et al. 2023**, Sports Med 53(3):649–665, PMID 36334240. Failure vs. non-failure had a trivial effect overall (ES 0.19). There was no advantage for momentary failure specifically (ES 0.12). The authors suggest the relationship is "non-linear": most of the benefit comes before true failure.
- **Martikainen et al. 2025**, J Sci Sport Exerc, doi 10.1007/s42978-025-00338-8 (Crossref abstract). Over two 5-week blocks, one group dropped its weekly target **from 4 RIR to 1 RIR** and the other stayed at **1 RIR** throughout. Strength and growth were similar, and the progressive group reported **lower session RPE**. This supports RIR that decreases across a block as a tolerable choice, not a better one.

## Q2. Should RIR change set volume? Autoregulation rules

**Nippard [primary + secondary]**
- **No Nippard source states a session-to-session rule** such as "a set reached 0 RIR, so lower next session's sets". [unconfirmed / appears not to exist]
- **Deloads** ("When And How To Deload (Science Explained)", YouTube, 3 Mar 2022, transcript: https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/when-and-how-to-deload-science-explained). This is older than 2024; I found no newer deload video.
  - Deload proactively every **4–8 weeks**, and more often the more advanced you are, because harder training creates more fatigue.
  - Cut volume by about **30–50%**, roughly 1–2 sets per exercise.
  - Ease off effort a little, e.g. from RPE 8–10 to RPE 6–8.
  - Simple version: "stay a few reps further from failure and do one less set per exercise."
  - Beginners in their first year usually don't need a deload.
- **Volume traded against effort:** his low-volume programs (Min-Max: 1–2 sets, often to failure) make up for fewer sets with higher effort [secondary, as above]. The reverse is not stated anywhere: he never says high-volume training must be done further from failure.

**The studies [primary abstracts]**
- **Refalo et al. 2024**, J Sports Sci 42(1):85–101, PMID 38393985. Failure caused more rep loss and velocity loss from set to set, so you get fewer reps in later sets. Even so, mean **volume load over 8 weeks was the same** as with 1–2 RIR, and growth was the same. The practical point is that 1–2 RIR keeps rep output steadier across sets with less acute fatigue.
- **Refalo et al. 2025**, Eur J Sport Sci 25(3):e12266, PMID 39960821. Failure raised discomfort and session RPE and made general feelings worse than 1–2 RIR. The authors suggest using this to set RIR so that adherence holds up.
- **Hickmott et al. 2022**, Sports Med Open 8:9, PMID 35038063. This is a meta-analysis of volume autoregulation by velocity loss. Higher velocity-loss thresholds (over 20–25%), meaning closer to failure and more reps, favored hypertrophy. Lower thresholds (25% or less) favored strength.

**RP stimulus/fatigue rules (not Nippard) [primary]**
Source: "In Defense of Set Increases Within the Hypertrophy Mesocycle", RP Strength, 1 Jul 2020. https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/in-defense-of-set-increases-within-the-hypertrophy-mesocycle
- RIR targets are lowered "every week or two", e.g. from 4 to 0 RIR across a mesocycle.
- Sets are adjusted by **signals of recovery and stimulus**, not by the RIR you hit:
  - Performance falling → don't add sets; cut them substantially.
  - Still sore when the muscle is trained again → don't add.
  - Healing just on time → hold.
  - Healing early with stable or rising performance → add.
  - Weak pump or little post-exercise weakness → consider adding.
  - Extreme pump or weakness → don't add.
  - Not recovering for weeks → drop the muscle to about MEV, or deload.
- "Autoregulated set additions and subtractions ... are superior to pre-planned increases."
- The RP app's exact algorithm is not public (the help-center page returned 403) [unconfirmed].

**Synthesis for the app (my inference, not a quoted rule)**
- After a session at 0–1 RIR: **hold** the set count.
- **Reduce** sets (by about 1 per exercise) only when there are signs of under-recovery: reps or load falling versus last time at the same RIR, or the muscle still sore at the next session.
- **Add** a set only when performance is improving and recovery comes early.
- This matches RP's logic, Refalo 2024 (failure doesn't reduce total volume over weeks), and Nippard's deload recipe.

## Q3. "80% effort" → RIR

- **Is it a Nippard concept?** No. I found no Nippard source that uses "80% effort" or "percent effort". He speaks only in RIR or RPE [searched; not found].
- **Mapping 1: 80% on the 10-point RPE scale.** The RIR-based RPE scale defines RPE 10 = 0 RIR, RPE 9 = 1 RIR, and so on. Source: Zourdos et al. 2016, J Strength Cond Res 30(1):267–275, PMID 26049792 [primary]. So **RPE 8 = 2 RIR**, whatever the rep range.
- **Mapping 2: 80% of the maximum reps possible with that load.** If N is the most reps you could do, RIR = 0.2 × N, which is 0.25 × the reps you actually do. This is simple arithmetic, not taken from a study. Pelland et al. 2022 (Sports Med 52:1461–1472, PMID 35247203) describe this kind of percentage prescription and note it produces widely varying RIR between people.

| Reps done (at 80% of max) | Implied max reps | Implied RIR | Inside Nippard's 0–3 / 1–2 window? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6–8 | 7.5–10 | ~1.5–2 | yes |
| 8–12 | 10–15 | ~2–3 | yes (upper edge) |
| 12–20 | 15–25 | ~3–5 | partly; 4–5 is beyond his "no more than 2–3 in the tank" |

- **Caveat on accuracy:** Halperin et al. 2022 (Sports Med 52:377–390, PMID 34542869) [primary] found that people **underpredict** reps to failure by about 1 rep on average. Accuracy is worse for sets over 12 reps and when the prediction is made far from failure. So a self-rated "80%" on a 15–20 rep set is likely even further from failure than the table suggests.
- **Recommended translation:**
  - Use **"80% effort" ≈ 2 RIR (RPE 8)** as the general target.
  - For **6–8 reps**: 1–2 RIR.
  - For **8–12 reps**: 2 RIR.
  - For **12–20 reps**: cap at **2–3 RIR**, not the 4–5 the arithmetic gives.
  - Optionally, take the last (or first) set of isolation exercises to 0–1 RIR, as Nippard does.
- This is my synthesis, built from Nippard's 0–3 and 1–2 RIR guidance and the RPE scale.

## Q4. Weekly sets per muscle and the link to effort

**Nippard [primary]**
- **9 Jul 2023**, "How To Build Muscle (Explained In 5 Levels)": "something around **10–20 sets per body part per week** seems to be a good range for most people and most muscles". He describes the response as an inverted U and says to train each muscle at least twice a week.
- **29 Apr 2024**, "How many sets do you need?" (https://jeffnippard.com/blogs/news/how-many-sets-do-you-need). This is his most recent written recommendation. Sets are counted by the muscles an exercise works:
  - **Beginner (under 12 months):** muscle can grow on 1–5 sets; aim for 4–8, working up to 8–12.
  - **Intermediate (1–4 years):** minimum 4–8; **target 8–15** across 3–5 days.
  - **Advanced (over 4 years):** 4–8 to maintain; **12–20** for progress; **15–25** for 1–2 lagging muscles at a time.
- **YouTube "How Much Training Volume Do You Really Need? (Science Explained)":** 10–20 sets per body part per week (https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/how-much-training-volume-do-you-really-need-science-explained) [secondary: transcript snippet; date not confirmed].
- **Pure Bodybuilding:** a 2026 third-party review lists about 10–20 working sets per muscle, with each muscle trained twice a week [secondary].

**Newest volume evidence [primary abstract]**
- **Pelland et al., "The Resistance Training Dose Response"**, Sports Med 2026 Feb; 56(2):481–505, published online 4 Dec 2025, PMID 41343037.
  - 67 studies, 2,058 participants.
  - Hypertrophy rises with weekly sets, with diminishing returns. Strength levels off sooner.
  - Frequency has a negligible effect on hypertrophy.
  - The best way of counting sets was **direct sets = 1, indirect sets = 0.5**.
  - Secondary write-ups say most of the data sits around 8 sets per week, and few studies go above about 25. That makes the high end of 10–20 an extrapolation.
  - I did not find a Nippard video specifically on this paper [unconfirmed].

**Is volume tied to effort? [primary]**
- **Implicitly.** His volume numbers assume "hard" sets: in 2023 he says most sets should end with no more than 2–3 RIR, and in 2024, 0–3 RIR.
- His 2024 volume article itself does not define a qualifying set. There is no effort condition on that page.
- **Low volume is paired with high effort:** Min-Max uses 1–2 sets often to failure, and his 100-day low-volume experiment took almost every set to failure [secondary].
- **App implication:** count a set toward the 10–20 range only if it ended at about 3 RIR or closer. Count indirect sets as 0.5, following Pelland 2025.
